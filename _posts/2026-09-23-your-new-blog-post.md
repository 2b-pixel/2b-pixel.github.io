## 23andMe

23andMe is a direct-to-consumer (DTC) genomics and biotechnology company, with its core product being an at-home saliva testing kit that utilizes DNA microarray sequencing to map a customer's genome. This allows users to access reports regarding their ancestry, health predispositions, and inherited traits.

* Who is the founder of the company?
  * 23andMe was co-founded by Anne Wojcicki, Linda Avey, and Paul Cusenza.
 
* When the company was founded? How much was the initial investment? What is the current revenue?
  * 23andMe was founded on April 24, 2006. The initial investment The company started with a 9 million dollar investment from backers including Google, Genentech, and New Enterprise Associates. The current revenue stands at 1.4 billion dollars today.
 
* What is their main business focus?
  * Their main business focus is selling genetic testing kits directly to consumers. Customers receive health risks, carrier status, traits, and family ancestry mapping.
  * Meanwhile, the core long-term value model relied on aggregating customer genetic data (with user consent) to build a biobank. 23andMe partnered with major pharmaceutical firms like GlaxoSmithKline (GSK) to license this database for drug discovery and early-stage drug development.
 
* What is the future success of the company?
  * 23andMe's future success is highly questioned due to its product. Unlike traditional subscription services, direct-to-consumer genetic testing is mostly a one-off purchase. Once customers discover their ancestry or health risks, they rarely purchase additional kits, seeing no reason to engage further.
