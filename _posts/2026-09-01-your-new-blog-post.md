## Heart Cancer

Heart cancer occurs when diseased cells grow out of control near the heart. There are two types of heart cancers: primary heart cancer (a rare condition where a tumor occurs within the heart itself) and secondary heart cancer (cancer spread to the heart from another affected site). Today’s post will focus on both types and will be specified respectively. 

* Frequencies of this type of cancer occur among the population?
    * Heart cancer is very rare, affecting 0.001-0.3% of the population. 
        * The rates with secondary heart cancer are still rare, but can be 20-30 times more frequent.

* How is this type of cancer typically identified/detected?
    * Heart cells do not divide and multiply unlike other cells, meaning if cancer cells are found in the heart—they typically may have been spread from elsewhere in the body. Types of cancers that can affect the heart include lung, breast, esophageal, kidney, leukemia and melanoma (This is associated with secondary heart cancer).
    * For detection the use Echocardiograms, CT scans, or MRIs are involved. 
    * Symptoms can include fluid buildup (around the heart), arrhythmia, chest pain, etc. However some people don’t show symptoms until much later as their condition progresses. 
        * This is seen in both primary and secondary heart cancer.

* Any known factors that give a person a high risk of getting that specific type of cancer?
    * The heart is typically immune to cancer cells. Though as mentioned earlier, certain types of cancer cells can affect the heart with secondary heart cancer. 
    * Other factors like smoking or AIDS can pose a risk 
        * Smoking is associated with secondary heart cancer while AIDS is associated with both (but commonly with secondary heart cancer).
    * Gene mutation is believed to be a cause for primary heart cancer, though research is still ongoing regarding this speculation.
    * Statistically, males between the ages 30-50 are slightly more likely to get heart cancer. Regardless, anyone can get this cancer.

* What stagings are used to describe this cancer? Or subtypes often used? 
    * There are 4 stages used to describe heart cancer.
        * Stage 1: A small cluster of cells form a tumor; typically, the patient won’t feel anything.
        * Stage 2: The tumor starts to grow. Symptoms like chest pain and shortness of breath may appear.
        * Stage 3: The tumor gets near heart tissue, both worsening symptoms and heart.
        * Stage 4: Eventually the tumor starts to spread into other areas. This makes it harder to treat, recover, and survive.


* What are the current available treatments for this cancer?
    * Treatment is based on certain factors like the tumor type, size, and location.
    * After removing the tumor, chemotherapy/radiation therapy is used to relieve symptoms and prevent future tumors.


* What is the average life expectancy for the patient?
    * Without surgery, the average life expectancy is 6 months.
    * With proper treatment people can live up to a year or more.
